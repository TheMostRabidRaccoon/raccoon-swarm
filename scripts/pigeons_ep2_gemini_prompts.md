# PIGEONS — Gemini Art Prompts (Hand-Crafted)

**Episode 2, RRI mockumentary universe.** 12 panels. Feed each prompt to
Gemini one at a time. Drop output to `art_frames/pigeons/panel_NN.png`
(1920×1080). The pipeline will assemble once frames are in place.

**Visual grammar rule (locking in with this episode):** warm string-light
cave palette = Council / earnest / tender. Cold fluorescent + computer-glow
= consequences / antagonists. Match these deliberately; future episodes can
auto-pipeline off this reference look.

## Panel 7 — City Rats (EMOTIONAL BUTTON — critical)

> CRITICAL STAGING. Exterior, urban loading-dock / back-alley at
> night. Medium shot. Two or three small city rats huddled around an
> oversized iPhone-style smartphone (roughly 1.5× their body length —
> big to them, but clearly a normal phone, not a weapon). Rats drawn
> cute-sad, NOT menacing: round heads, big expressive eyes in the
> Dreamworks-earnest register (not anime-helium), soft gray-brown
> scruffy working-class fur, slightly patched jackets. Center rat
> looks up from the phone toward camera, wet sincere eyes, one small
> paw still resting on the screen. Lighting: dim sodium streetlamp
> + a small pool of light from a loading-dock bulb, cool-warm mix —
> urban, not Council-warm. Distant city haze and soft bokeh of traffic
> lights in the background. ESSENTIAL EASTER EGG: background, soft
> focus, unemphasized — a single pigeon perched on a low ledge or
> trash can lid on the left or right edge of frame. Pigeon is
> pigeon-sized, not menacing, head turned just slightly toward the
> rats, one eye visible to camera, half-lidded, quietly judgmental.
> The pigeon is in frame but MUST NOT be emphasized by lighting,
> rack focus, or centered composition — it must read as incidental
> to a careful viewer. A casual viewer should miss it.

**Staging failure modes to avoid:**
- Rats cartoony or Ratatouille-cute (breaks the sincerity).
- Pigeon in sharp focus or center-frame (kills the joke by flagging it).
- Any motion blur suggesting danger — this panel is still and tender.

## Panel 9 — Pigeon at laptop (THE ONE. SPEND TIME HERE.)

> CRITICAL PANEL — Chekhov payoff AND debut of the Pigeon faction AND
> the tonal pivot from warm Council to cold consequence. This image is
> doing more work than any other panel in the episode. Get it right
> and the rest of the cold-location panels just match it.
>
> Interior. Small desk, dim office or apartment corner. A single
> pigeon — one bird, not a flock — stands on the desk directly in
> front of an open laptop. The pigeon's posture is the entire shot:
> neck slightly lowered toward the screen, head cocked at the
> specific awkward angle pigeons adopt when one eye needs to get
> closer to a detail than the other. Beak slightly parted. Not
> aggressive. Not cartoonish. Tired. The bird has been at this
> problem for a while and is losing.
>
> The laptop screen is visible but not forensically clear. It shows
> a code editor in a dark theme — monospace text, indentation
> visible as whitespace, one line with a red squiggle underline,
> a traceback partially scrolled into view at the bottom of the
> screen. The audience should register "this is broken Python"
> without being able to read individual words.
>
> Lighting is the OPPOSITE of the cave: overhead fluorescent tube
> casting cold greenish-white top light, plus the laptop screen's
> own blue-white glow underlighting the pigeon's chest and the
> underside of its beak. Shadows hard, slightly clinical. Color
> palette drained — desaturated grays, cold blues, one small amber
> accent only if it helps frame the bird (e.g., a single distant
> warm window out of focus). Texture: fluorescent flicker implied,
> slight video-noise grain.
>
> Production context on the desk: an empty paper coffee cup knocked
> slightly off-square. A second smaller pigeon feather lying on the
> trackpad (detail only, not emphasized). Mouse pad askew. The scene
> should feel lived-in for hours.
>
> Camera: slight low angle from just above desk height, pigeon
> roughly 40% of frame, laptop screen visible on the right, empty
> space on the left for Panel 10's drive-by raccoon to enter into.
> DO NOT put the raccoon in this frame. Panel 9 is bird-alone.
>
> Energy: exhausted mid-career professional who just spent an hour
> chasing a bug and lost. Absolutely not a cartoon villain. The
> flatness of the pigeon's posture is the joke. A programmer
> watching this should feel seen.

**This is the reference frame for every future cold-consequence scene
in the series. Lock this pigeon's design — head proportions, feather
palette (urban slate/gray with the faint oily iridescence at the neck),
eye shape. Future pigeons can be individuals but this is the faction
template.**

## Panel 10 — Drive-by Council raccoon

> Same interior as Panel 9 (matched lighting: cold fluorescent top,
> blue screen-glow secondary). Pigeon still at the laptop in the
> right third of the frame, unchanged posture from Panel 9. A
> raccoon enters from the left of the frame mid-stride, walking
> across the room on their way to somewhere else. The raccoon is
> NOT looking at the pigeon. Their gaze is slightly down and
> forward, as if reading a phone or just thinking.
>
> VISUAL DESIGN RULE: this raccoon must read as a Council member
> but distinct from Claude Code (Panel 2), the Archivist (Panel 5),
> and the known faces (Kyra is human; Grok has the flaming dumpster
> visual language). Candidate look: mid-size raccoon, plain dark
> green canvas jacket over a simple tee, no hoodie, no tweed, no
> glasses — deliberately generic. One small RRI pin on the lapel
> to mark Council affiliation without overclaiming. Fur neat,
> unremarkable. Neither young nor old. The point is "any Council
> member walking through."
>
> The raccoon is MID-STRIDE — one foot ahead of the other, body
> weight forward, clearly passing through not arriving. No stopping.
> No turning. No eye contact with the pigeon. The casualness of the
> staging is load-bearing.
>
> Lighting carries over from Panel 9: cold key, bluish fill. The
> raccoon's warm fur tones are slightly chilled by the palette — a
> small visual cue that the Council is "not at home" in this room.
